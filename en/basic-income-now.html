Preventing poverty, realising democracy, enabling freedom

Stefan Füsers

Why basic income has never been as urgent as it is now

Unconditional basic income is one of the simplest ideas there is. Everyone receives an amount each month that is enough to live on, without conditions and without anything in return. The idea has been around for decades, and its reasons have stayed the same. What is new is that artificial intelligence makes it more urgent than ever.

Three reasons that have always applied

Preventing poverty. Anyone who receives a sufficient basic income is not poor. Poverty is thereby abolished, simply by definition. It has always been affordable. National income in Germany was around 3.4 trillion euros in 2025.1 That is a good 40,000 euros for every person in the country. If only half of it were distributed evenly, everyone would receive almost 1,700 euros a month. So the money is there.

Realising democracy. We live in a democracy, yet money today often has more power than a vote. Our free democratic order has long been above all a capitalist one, and it is free especially for those who can afford it. A basic income is the basic equipment with which everyone can stand up for what matters to them in our society. Today only a few can, and that has little to do with fair and equal opportunities.

Enabling freedom. Anyone who is secure chooses their work of their own accord. Nobody has to take a job just because the rent would otherwise go unpaid. Employers and employees meet as equals and negotiate what the work is worth to others. Anyone who prefers to work for themselves can do that too.

What is changing now

Artificial intelligence is changing the labour market quickly and fundamentally. Knowledge work, the so-called white-collar jobs, is hit first. Anyone working with AI becomes many times more productive. I experience this myself. Research, texts, presentations and websites come together with AI in a fraction of the time. This leap has come within a few months.

Mathematics shows how big it is. In October 2026 OpenAI published solutions to more than 370 open mathematical problems.2 Experts are still checking them, and some solutions have been withdrawn. Still, the comparison is worth making. In the past, one of these problems was solved every few years, and each solution made technology and the economy more productive for decades. Now hundreds come at once. We can hardly imagine today what this will mean in three or ten years.

We do not have to build AI ourselves for this progress. The results are open and can be built into every area of the economy. Anyone who claims that Europe has no AI of its own and therefore nothing to distribute is wrong. Productivity is arriving here too. We only have to embrace it and prepare society for it.

At the same time, less and less work for others is needed. Many processes in companies can gradually be automated. This is already visible in the tech sector. Microsoft cut around 9,000 jobs in 2025 and another 4,800 in 2026 while investing billions in AI.3 Automation starts at the top with brain work and works its way down until, with robotics, physical work, the blue-collar jobs, can be automated too. This will not happen everywhere or overnight. The relationship between employers and employees will continue for decades. But the direction is clear, and it cannot be reversed.

A long line

This development is not new. The chart shows it for Germany over two hundred years. Since the second half of the 19th century, value creation and capital stock have been growing while the volume of work per person has been falling. We produce more and more with less and less work, and capital is becoming ever more important for productivity. AI extends this line and accelerates it. In future, productivity will depend above all on chips, memory and computing power, that is, on capital.

A freedom that could not be greater

In fact, this fulfils what we have been working towards since the first spear. Tools take work off our hands. If, with today’s methods, we can automate a large part of what we need to live, a freedom emerges that could not be greater. Our task is to use it and to value it. We can direct it towards living together non-violently and democratically, with other people and with all other life on this earth, and for the long term.

That is why we need basic income. Anyone who no longer finds work for others and has no capital to live on will otherwise end up in poverty. Capital keeps gaining influence over democracy, and freedom shrinks for many. It is about the same three values as always. Only what we can gain is greater today than ever before.

Basic income now

An unconditional basic income has never been as urgent as it is today. Even Elon Musk now talks about it. We are still well enough off to build a new world from a reasonably comfortable position. This window will not stay open for long. It is time to retire the phrase “He who does not work, neither shall he eat” and to embrace the new world.

Financing is not the obstacle. In the financial crisis and in the pandemic we saw that states and central banks can create money on a large scale in times of crisis. A basic income can be financed through taxes on large fortunes and capital income. There is also a second way. Newly created money, distributed evenly to everyone, also works as redistribution because it devalues large fortunes in relative terms. What is missing is the political will.

An impulse contribution by Stefan Füsers
